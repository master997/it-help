# Lost or stolen laptop

> Channel names and contact details below are placeholders — swap them for the
> real ones.

**Report it first, look for it second.** Everything else on this page can wait;
that can't. Reporting takes thirty seconds and is reversible — if you find the
laptop in your other bag an hour later, nothing is lost. An unreported laptop
sitting in a taxi for a day is a different problem entirely.

Nobody is in trouble for losing a laptop. It happens several times a year at
any company. The only thing that makes it worse is waiting.

## I've lost my laptop

1. **Post in #it-help now.** Say: lost laptop, roughly where and when, and
   whether you think it was stolen. Don't wait until you've finished
   searching.
2. **If it's outside working hours, call the on-call number** in your welcome
   pack rather than posting. Out of hours, a Slack message may sit unread until
   morning — which is exactly the window that matters.
3. **Then go and look for it.** Retrace your route, call the venue, check lost
   property. Most laptops turn up.

Once you've reported it, we lock it remotely and sign it out of your accounts.
That happens whether or not the laptop is switched on — the moment it next
connects to the internet, it locks.

## It's been stolen

If your laptop was stolen, nicked, taken, or robbed — from a bag, a car, a cafe
table —
the first move is the same as a loss: **post in #it-help immediately** (or call
the on-call number out of hours) and say clearly that it was stolen rather than
mislaid, because we treat the two differently. We'll lock it remotely straight
away.

Then:

1. **Report it to the police** and get a crime reference number. In the UK you
   can do this online at [police.uk](https://www.police.uk) — you don't need to
   attend a station.
2. **Send the reference number to IT.** We need it for insurance, and if it's a
   work-related theft the company handles that side, not you.
3. **If it was taken with your phone**, tell us that too. Losing both at once
   means your 2FA codes are gone as well, and we'll deal with that first so you
   can still get into your accounts.

## I might get it back — do I still report it?

Yes. Report it, then keep looking.

Reporting is not a decision you can't undo. If it turns up, we unlock it in a
couple of minutes and you carry on. There's no paperwork, no incident, no
follow-up meeting.

The question worth asking yourself isn't "will I probably find it" — it's "am I
certain nobody else could have picked it up in the meantime."

## What happens after I report it

1. We lock the laptop and sign it out of email, Slack, Drive and everything
   else. Your accounts stay yours — it's the device that's cut off, not you.
2. We check whether anything was accessed after you lost it. Usually nothing
   was.
3. We arrange a replacement. If you're in the office there's usually a spare
   the same day; remote, it's a courier and typically two working days.
4. Your files are fine. Everything in Google Drive is on the server, not the
   laptop.

Anything saved only to your Desktop or Downloads and never opened in Drive is
the one thing that may not survive. That's worth knowing now rather than
later — it's the reason we ask people to work out of Drive.

## I found it

Post in #it-help and we'll unlock it. Bring it in — or if you're remote, we'll
do it on a call.

Before you use it again, tell us where you found it. If it was somewhere out
of your control for a while (a taxi, a bar, a train's lost property), we'll
wipe and reinstall it before you carry on. That's not a punishment or a
judgement about you — it's that we can't tell from the outside whether anything
was plugged into it while it was gone, and reinstalling takes an hour while
finding out the hard way takes considerably longer.
