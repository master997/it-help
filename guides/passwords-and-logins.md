# Passwords and logins

> Tool names and URLs below are placeholders — swap them for the real ones.

## I can't get into 1Password

1. Check you're using your **work** account, not a personal one. If you had
   1Password before joining, the app may be opening your personal vault —
   click your account name at the top and switch.
2. If it's asking for a **Secret Key** you don't have, that's in the email
   1Password sent when your account was created. Search your inbox for
   "1Password Emergency Kit".
3. Can't find that email — post in #it-help and we'll send a new invite. We
   can't recover your existing one, but a fresh invite gets you back in.

Don't reset 1Password by creating a new account. You'll end up with an empty
vault and lose access to everything shared with you.

## I've forgotten my Mac password

We can't look this up — it's stored on your laptop, not on a server. But we
can get you back in.

1. Restart the Mac. At the login screen, wait a few seconds and it should
   offer **Reset it using your Apple ID** or a question mark by the password
   box. If your Mac is set up with your work Google account, follow that
   prompt.
2. If nothing appears, bring the laptop to IT. Don't try guessing repeatedly —
   after several attempts it locks for progressively longer.
3. If you're remote, post in #it-help and we'll book fifteen minutes on a
   call. It's fixable, it just needs a person.

## A site is asking for a 6-digit code and I don't have it

That's two-factor authentication. The code lives in 1Password, not your text
messages.

1. Open **1Password** and find the login for that site.
2. Look for a field called **one-time password**. The 6-digit code is there
   and changes every thirty seconds.
3. Copy it and paste it in quickly — if it expires, wait for the next one
   rather than reusing the old one.

If there's no such field on that login, 2FA was probably set up on your phone
instead. Check your Authenticator app.

## I've lost my phone and can't get my 2FA codes

Post in #it-help and say clearly that it's a 2FA lockout. This one we treat as
urgent, because it locks you out of everything at once.

Have your laptop with you when we get to you — the fastest fix uses a session
that's already signed in, before it expires.

## I think someone else has my password

Tell us straight away in #it-help, before you try to fix it yourself.

Say what you think happened — clicked a link, typed it into a site that looked
wrong, shared it with someone. Nobody is in trouble for reporting this. Every
company deals with it, and the only version that causes real damage is the one
nobody mentions for a week.

While you wait for us:

1. Change that password in 1Password if you can still get in.
2. Don't delete the suspicious email or message — we'll want to see it to
   check whether anyone else got the same one.

## How do I make a good password?

Let 1Password generate it. That's the whole answer — click the field, choose
**Generate password**, save it.

The only password you should ever create yourself is your Mac login, since
that's the one you type from memory. Three or four unrelated words with a
number is both stronger and easier to remember than a short jumble of symbols.
